# Writing Guidelines (Anti-AI-Slop)

Tactical line-level rules for generation and QA, adapted from
[no-ai-slop](https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop) (Peter Yang, MIT licensed).
This is separate from `context.md`: that file is brand voice, pillars, and
published post history; this file is the checklist of specific words and
patterns that make writing read as AI-generated.

## Banned words - cut outright
delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust,
cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, this is huge, this changes
everything, tapestry, realm, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate,
paramount, transformative, elevate, embark, supercharge, harness,
ever-evolving.

## Often-empty adverbs - cut unless they carry real emphasis or uncertainty
just, literally, honestly, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally,
importantly, crucially, inherently, inevitably.

## Often-empty phrases - cut when they delay the point
it's worth noting, it's important to note, at the end of the day, when it
comes to, at its core, in today's world, in the age of, in the world of, the
reality is, the truth is, in terms of, with regard to, in order to, going
forward, let's dive in.

## Patterns to avoid

- **Binary contrasts.** "It's not X. It's Y." State Y directly.
- **Throat-clearing openers.** "Here's the thing," "Let me be clear," "The
  uncomfortable truth is." Cut and state the point.
- **Faux-insight setups.** "What nobody tells you," "The part everyone
  misses." Cut the setup, make the claim stand alone.
- **Colon reveals.** "The best part: it learns." Rewrite as a plain sentence.
- **Superficial analysis.** Trailing "-ing" clauses that pretend to explain
  meaning: "highlighting the team's commitment," "underscoring its impact."
- **Importance puffery.** "Marks a pivotal moment," "underscores its
  significance." State the fact, let the reader judge if it matters.
- **Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree," "studies show" with no named
  source. Name the source or cut the claim.
- **Fake-strong verbs.** "Serves as a centralized hub" instead of a plain
  "is" or "has."
- **Synonym cycling.** Rotating "the agent / the assistant / the tool" for
  the same thing instead of repeating the clear word.
- **Negative listing.** "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Just say Z.
- **Dramatic fragmentation.** "X. And Y. And Z." or "That's it. That's the
  whole thing." Use complete sentences.
- **Robotic rhythm.** Repeated sentence shapes and stacked punchy fragments.
  Vary sentence length.
- **Rhetorical setups.** "What if I told you...", "Plot twist:",
  self-answered "Question? Answer." pairs.
- **Fake-profound kickers.** A final cute metaphor or mic-drop line tacked
  onto the end. End on the clearest concrete point instead.
- **Summary-recap endings.** "In conclusion," "Ultimately," or a closing
  paragraph that just restates the piece.
- **Formatting slop.** Emoji in headings, bold sprinkled mid-sentence,
  bullets where two sentences of prose would read better.
- **Em dashes as a rhythm crutch.** Already a hard rule in `context.md` -
  reinforced here: don't reach for em dashes by default.

## Core principles

- Be concrete and specific. "The integration improved efficiency" becomes
  "The integration cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 4."
- Use active voice. Don't let inanimate things do human verbs.
- Make verbs do the work: "made a decision" becomes "decided."
- Lead with the point when a setup adds nothing.
- Vary sentence and paragraph shape - don't make every sentence the same
  length and structure.
